What QOZ status would actually require

General information, not advice. Checklist of the main federal rules; counsel must confirm how they apply.

  • The investment must come through a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF): a corporation or partnership that self-certifies on IRS Form 8996 and holds at least 90% of its assets in qualified opportunity zone property.
  • The business must be a Qualified Opportunity Zone Business. That includes the test that at least 70 percent of its tangible property is qualified opportunity zone business property, plus the other QOZB tests (at least 50% of gross income from active business in the zone, a substantial portion of intangible property used there, limits on nonqualified financial property, and no excluded "sin" businesses).
  • Existing buildings generally need substantial improvement, roughly doubling the basis of the building (not the land) within 30 months.
  • This tract is flagged as comprised entirely of a rural area. For rural QOZs the substantial-improvement threshold is 50% of basis instead of 100% (Pub. L. 119-21; IRS Notice 2025-50).
  • The working capital safe harbor can protect cash held under a written plan for up to 31 months.
  • Being in the tract is necessary and nowhere near sufficient. The fund, the business, the property and the timing all have to qualify.
  • Timing: this tract's 2018 round designation ends Dec 31, 2028 and the tract is not in the 2027 round. Have counsel confirm how that affects any investment timeline.
